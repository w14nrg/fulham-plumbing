Common signs

  • Drips under sinks or behind appliances
  • Damp patches or stains on ceilings or walls
  • Water meter movement when all outlets are closed
  • Soft, lifting or swollen flooring
  • Hissing or running-water sounds
  • A sudden pressure drop

Water can travel along joists, pipes and the back of panels before it appears, so the visible mark is not always directly below the failed fitting.

Before we arrive

If water is escaping now, turn it off at the internal stopcock if you can do so safely. Keep clear of wet lights, sockets and appliances.

Common causes

Failed joints

Compression, push-fit and threaded joints can loosen, corrode or lose their seal. A small weep can become a larger leak when pressure or movement changes.

Damaged pipework

Copper can corrode or be pierced by a screw or nail. Older steel sections can rust internally until a pinhole opens.

Valves and connectors

Flexible tap tails, isolation valves and appliance valves contain seals that age. A failure may leak only while the fitting is in use.

Overflowing fittings

A cistern or storage tank can keep filling when its float valve fails, sending water to an overflow or into surrounding finishes.

What we check and do

Isolate and make safe

We identify the correct stopcock or local isolation valve and reduce the water flow before opening anything up.

Trace the source

We use visible evidence, meter behaviour and pressure checks. With permission, access may include removing a panel or lifting a floorboard.

Repair the failed part

The damaged fitting, valve or pipe section is repaired or replaced rather than simply dried and hidden.

Test under pressure

Water is restored carefully and the repair is watched. We explain what failed and what making-good may still be needed.

Finding a concealed leak sometimes needs access through a removable panel or floorboard. We agree that first. Plastering, decorating and flooring repairs are separate.

In Fulham homes

Local context matters.

In converted houses and mansion blocks, a wet ceiling can start in the flat above. We check the affected side, narrow down the source and explain what a neighbour, landlord or managing agent needs to know.

Questions

Common questions

What should I do first?

Turn off the nearest safe isolation valve or the internal stopcock if water is escaping. Move belongings away, catch what you safely can and keep clear of electrical fittings. Then call and describe where the water is appearing.

How do you find a hidden leak?

We work from meter movement, pressure, staining, sound and the position of nearby fittings. Finishes are opened only when there is a clear reason and with your agreement.

Can you come today?

Same-day attendance is possible during working hours when there is space in the diary. Send a photo and postcode or call, and we will give an honest arrival option.

Will you need to lift floorboards?

Sometimes. If the evidence points below a floor, lifting an accessible board can be the least destructive way to confirm the source.

Do you repair the ceiling afterwards?

The plumbing repair is our part of the job. Drying, plastering, decorating and flooring repairs are separate.
