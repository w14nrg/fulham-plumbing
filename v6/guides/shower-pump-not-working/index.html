Shower pump not working: what to check

By Fulham PlumbingLast updated 2026-10-01

Short answer

Do not open the electrical casing. Check whether the pump has water available, whether isolation valves are open and whether the fault is no-start, weak flow, noise or cycling. Those symptoms point in different directions.

First, safety

A shower pump combines water and electricity, so the safe DIY checks are external. Do not remove covers, bypass switches or handle wet wiring. If there is visible leakage, isolate the water supplies and the existing electrical supply only if both can be reached safely.

Before assuming the pump is dead, make sure the storage tank has water and that nearby service valves are open. A dry or starved pump can be damaged by repeated attempts to run it.

Pump won't start

A pump that remains silent may not be sensing flow, may have an electrical supply problem or may be unsuitable for the available natural flow. Negative-head systems are designed for situations where gravity flow is too small to trigger an ordinary pump.

Try another pumped outlet if the system has one. If nothing starts the pump, stop there. Repeated switching will not repair a failed flow switch and opening the case is not a safe homeowner check.

Pump runs but flow is weak

Weak output can come from blocked inlet filters, partly closed valves, kinked hoses, air or a restriction before the pump. Replacing the motor without checking the feed can leave exactly the same problem.

The storage tank also needs enough water and correctly arranged connections. If the pump draws air, performance drops and noise often increases at the same time.

Pump is very noisy

Some sound is normal, but grinding, rattling or a new high-pitched note can indicate worn bearings, cavitation or vibration through the floor. Flexible hoses and mounting can make a large difference to transmitted noise.

Noise that starts only after a few seconds can point towards restricted supply. A plumber checks the feed and filters before concluding that the internal moving parts are worn.

Pump starts and stops on its own

Cycling when no outlet is open can be caused by a small leak, a failing switch or pressure change in the connected pipework. A dripping shower valve may be enough to wake some pumps repeatedly.

Do not ignore this behaviour because repeated cycling shortens pump life. The source needs to be found rather than silenced by switching the pump off between showers.

Leaks around the pump

Water may come from a flexible hose, union or the pump body itself. Dry the outside, then observe carefully from a safe position to see which connection becomes wet first.

A leaking connection can often be repaired without replacing the complete pump. A cracked or leaking pump body is different and normally makes replacement more sensible.

Repair or replace?

Filters, switches, hoses, airlocks and installation issues can sometimes be corrected. Worn bearings, damaged bodies and repeated internal failure make replacement more likely.

The replacement should match the system type, number of outlets and required head. Brand alone is not a specification; the pump needs to suit the stored-water arrangement.

What to note before booking a repair

A short video can be very useful because pump faults are often about behaviour as much as appearance. Record what happens when the shower is opened: whether the motor starts immediately, hesitates, pulses, runs continuously or stays silent. If the sound changes once the water has been running for a few seconds, mention that too.

Photograph the pump label without removing any cover. The model number and pressure rating help identify the correct replacement and the manufacturer's installation requirements. Also photograph the flexible hoses and nearby valves so the plumber can see how the pump is connected before arriving.

If more than one outlet uses the pump, say which outlets still work. One weak shower with another pumped outlet working normally points more towards a local shower restriction. Everything being weak or silent makes the pump, its feed or the common valves more likely.

  • Model and pressure rating from the external label.
  • Which outlets are pumped and which still work.
  • Whether the fault is silence, noise, weak flow or cycling.
  • Any recent tank, valve or pipework changes.

Why the water supply to the pump matters

A pump can only move the water that reaches it. If the storage tank is low, a feed valve is restricted or an inlet hose is collapsing, the motor may sound as though it is failing when it is actually being starved. That is why the supply side is checked before condemning the pump.

The same applies after work in the loft or airing cupboard. A valve that was closed for maintenance may not have been reopened fully, or air may have entered the pipework. Those are installation and supply faults, not reasons to replace an otherwise serviceable pump.

When to call a plumber

Call when the pump will not start after basic external checks, when it leaks, cycles by itself, becomes very noisy or needs electrical covers removed.

Questions

Can I reset a shower pump?

Some units have simple external reset procedures, but that depends on the model. Never open the case or bypass a safety device. Use the manufacturer's instructions for the exact pump.

How long do shower pumps last?

There is no single reliable lifespan. Water quality, installation, run time, vibration and whether the pump is starved of water all affect service life.

Why does my pump run when nothing is on?

A small leak, pressure change or failing switch can make the pump think an outlet has opened. The connected shower valve and pipework need checking.
